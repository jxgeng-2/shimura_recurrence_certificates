RECURRENCE FORMULAS FOR CM EXPANSIONS ON GENUS-ONE
AND GENUS-TWO SHIMURA CURVES
Abstract. We establish a general recurrence relation for normalized local expansions of weight-
two eigenforms on Shimura curves. The recurrence follows from a Schwarzian identity associated
with a local primitive of the eigenform differential. We then determine the recurrence explicitly
at selected CM points for all weight-two eigenforms on Shimura curves of genus one and two and
the complete Taylor expansions of eigenforms conditionally. Atkin–Lehner descent expresses the
Schwarzian function in a genus-zero quotient coordinate, yielding a closed coupled differential
system. Local pole constraints, explicit algebraic models, and known Schwarzian equations
reduce the determination of this system to finitely many parameters, which are recovered from
initial expansion coefficients. We present recurrence data for twelve eigenforms on the nine
Shimura curves of trivial level and discriminants (14,15,21,26,33,34,38,46,58).
1.Introduction
1.1.Explicit computation on Shimura curves.Let XN =XN0 (1)be the Shimura curve of
trivial level associated with the indefinite quaternion algebra overQof discriminantN. Explicit
equations, uniformization maps, and evaluations of modular forms are fundamental tools for
studying the arithmetic of these Shimura curves. The absence of cusps onXN makes these
computations particularly challenging, since the usual methods on classical modular curves based
on Fourier expansions at cusps are unavailable.
When XN has small genus, one can often obtain explicit models by combining coverings with
arithmetic information about CM points. For example, González–Rotger [4, 5] determine models
of genus-one and genus-one Shimura curves by studying their elliptic Jacobians and elliptic
quotients. Guo–Yang [11] give equations for the hyperelliptic Shimura curves by constructing
modular functions with prescribed CM divisors as Borcherds forms. Their results give Table 1,
which lists the modelsy2 =FN(x)for Shimura curvesXN of genus one and two, together with
their eigendifferentials, which will be used in this paper.
Powerful tools enabling explicit computations on Shimura curves are the Schwarzian derivative
and the associated Schwarzian differential equations. Elkies [2] introduced them to study the local
inversez of the covering mapp :H→X N. Together with ramification data and Atkin–Lehner
symmetries, Elkies was able to compute covering maps and their local inverses numerically and
algebraically and construct equations. Tu [8] systematically determines Schwarzian equations
for a specified family of genus-zero Shimura quotients and obtains the Schwarzian derivative
for many small-genus curves. Yang [14] develops a method for computing a basis for the space
of modular formsSk(Γ)and Hecke operators on genus-zero Shimura curvesXN =H/Γusing
solutions to the Schwarzian differential equations.
The explicit analytic computation of modular forms on a compact Shimura curve, where
neither cusps nor q-expansions are available, is another fundamental problem in computations
on Shimura curves. One can instead study a Taylor expansion at an interior pointτ∈X N, such
as a CM point, as proposed by Zagier [15, Section 6.3]. See also Voight–Willis [9, Section 2]. For

All numerical methods mentioned above work well for a small number of initial coefficientsbn
but suffer from computational limitations if one wants to compute more. For example, it is rather
easy to computebn up ton = 100for the weight-two eigenform14a using the above method,
but it is completely out of reach to computebn up ton = 10000. As a result, the computed

Voight–Willis [9, Remark 2.6] raise the problem of finding explicit recursions for CM expansions
by studying the differential structure of modular forms on Shimura curves. Such recursions can
easily determine all subsequent coefficients exactly from finitely many initial coefficients. Zagier
[15, Proposition 28, p. 88] combines Ramanujan’s differential identities with modular differential
operators to obtain a polynomial recursion for the expansion of the Eisenstein seriesE4 at the
CM point of discriminant−4. Baba–Granath [1] adapt this approach to modular forms on
the Shimura curveX6: they use the Schwarzian differential equation of a modular function to
establish a differential system for generators of the modular-form ring, from which they derive
explicit recurrences for the expansions.
